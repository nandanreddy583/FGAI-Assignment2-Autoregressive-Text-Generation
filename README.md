## 1. Problem statement and objective

Natural-language generation is the task of producing fluent, contextually appropriate text. This assignment implements and compares four recurrent neural architectures for **word-level autoregressive next-word prediction**:

- **Simple RNN**
- **Bidirectional RNN** (`Bidirectional(SimpleRNN)`)
- **LSTM**
- **GRU**

Given a sequence of words, each model predicts the probability distribution of the *next* word; text is then generated by repeatedly sampling from that distribution and appending the sampled word. The objectives are to:

1. Preprocess a textual dataset and build the input–target sequences required for autoregressive modelling.
2. Train all four models under **comparable experimental conditions**.
3. Evaluate them **quantitatively** (loss, perplexity, prediction accuracy, training time) and **qualitatively** (coherence, diversity, contextual consistency, repetition).
4. Generate text from identical seeds at **three sampling temperatures** and analyse the effect of temperature on generation quality.
5. Identify the **most effective model** for the chosen dataset.

## 2. Dataset description and source

**Dataset:** *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* by Lewis Carroll.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Source | Project Gutenberg eBook #11 — https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11 |
| Plain-text URL | https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11/pg11.txt |
| Domain | English narrative fiction (children's literature) |
| Size (cleaned) | ~150,000 characters / ~30,000 tokens |
| Style | Varied sentences, dialogue, recurring characters, punctuation-rich |

The Project Gutenberg license header/footer are removed before analysis. The dataset is freely available at a stable public URL, making the experiment fully reproducible.

**Preprocessing**

1. Lower-casing.
2. Regex tokenization `[a-z]+(?:'[a-z]+)?|[.,!?;:]` — keeps words (including contractions such as *alice's*) and the punctuation marks `.,!?;:` as standalone tokens.
3. **Chronological** split: first 80 % → train, next 10 % → validation, last 10 % → test (avoids leakage between overlapping sliding windows).
4. Vocabulary built from the **training split only**: tokens with frequency ≥ 2, capped at 5,000, plus reserved `<pad>` (0) and `<oov>` (1).
5. **Input–target construction:** every consecutive window of `SEQ_LEN = 15` tokens is an input and the following token is the target (many-to-one autoregressive formulation).

## 3. Models implemented

All four models share an identical stack; only the recurrent layer differs:

```
Input (15 tokens) -> Embedding(vocab, 64) -> Recurrent(128) -> Dense(vocab, softmax)
```

| Model | Recurrent layer | Optimiser | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple RNN | `SimpleRNN(128)` | Adam | sparse categorical cross-entropy |
| Bidirectional RNN | `Bidirectional(SimpleRNN(128))` | Adam | sparse categorical cross-entropy |
| LSTM | `LSTM(128)` | Adam | sparse categorical cross-entropy |
| GRU | `GRU(128)` | Adam | sparse categorical cross-entropy |

**Comparable conditions:** identical sequence length (15), embedding size (64), hidden units (128), optimiser (Adam), batch size (128), loss, epochs (10), random seed and data splits for every model.

## 6. Model comparison and evaluation

- **GRU is the best model overall** — lowest validation/test loss, lowest test perplexity (**65.71**) and joint-highest test accuracy (**0.2098**). Its generation is the most coherent, contextual and the least repetitive at low temperature.
- **LSTM** is a close second on accuracy (0.2095) but has higher perplexity (77.23) and is the **slowest** to train (104 s) due to heavier gating.
- **Simple RNN** is the **fastest** (43.5 s, ~2.4× faster than LSTM) and surprisingly competitive on perplexity (73.74), but it captures long-range dependencies poorly and its generations drift into loops more quickly.
- **Bidirectional RNN** overfits: it has the **lowest training loss (3.482)** but the **highest validation/test loss (4.411/4.457)** and the weakest accuracy (0.1897). It is also conceptually the least suitable architecture for *autoregressive* generation, since its backward pass assumes access to future context that does not exist during generation.

**Qualitative comparison**

| Criterion | Simple RNN | Bidirectional RNN | LSTM | GRU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coherence | Moderate | Moderate | High | High |
| Diversity | High (at T ≥ 1) | High | High | High |
| Contextual consistency | Moderate | Moderate | High | High |
| Repetition (T = 0.5) | Low–moderate | High | Highest | **Lowest** |

## 7. Sampling-temperature analysis

Generation was performed at **T = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5** from two identical seeds.

| Temperature | Coherence | Diversity (Distinct-1) | Repetition (3-grams) | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | High — grammatical, on-theme | Low (≈ 0.34–0.42) | Highest (up to 0.27) | Factual / safe text |
| 1.0 | Balanced | Medium (≈ 0.65–0.75) | Very low (≈ 0–0.01) | **General-purpose generation** |
| 1.5 | Lower — semantic drift | Highest (≈ 0.80–0.86) | ~None | Creative / exploratory |

Temperature trades **coherence against diversity**. Low temperature sharpens the softmax, favouring frequent, safe words (fluent but repetitive). High temperature flattens it, unlocking rarer words and novel phrasing at the expense of grammaticality and topical consistency. **T ≈ 1.0** is the recommended sweet spot for this dataset; **T = 0.5** is preferable when grammatical soundness matters most.

---

## 8. Conclusion — best-performing model

**The GRU is the most effective autoregressive generator for the *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* dataset.** It achieves the best quantitative results (lowest test loss and perplexity, highest accuracy), the lowest repetition at low temperature, and among the most coherent and contextually consistent text — while training ~5 % faster than the LSTM and using ~7 % fewer parameters. The LSTM is a strong runner-up for more conservative sampling, the SimpleRNN is the best speed/quality compromise for rapid experimentation, and the Bidirectional RNN, while capable during training, is the least appropriate choice for causal, left-to-right text generation.
